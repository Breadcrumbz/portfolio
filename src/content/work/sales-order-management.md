## Context

I led discovery and process reengineering work for a complex SD-WAN sales order management journey at a Tier 1 telecommunications provider.

The goal was to move from fragmented, manual hand offs to a clearer future state fulfilment workflow that could support automation and AI-assisted decision making while retaining appropriate human oversight.

This was more than a process mapping exercise. It required turning incomplete and distributed stakeholder knowledge into a shared view of how work moved from quote authoring to order provisioning and exception handling. That evidence then informed a feasible transformation pathway.

## Challenge

The existing sales order process had evolved across multiple teams and ordering roles. No single group held an end-to-end view of the journey from a customer quote to a live provisioned service order.

This created many connected problems:

- Manual, email-based hand offs slowed progress and made ownership difficult to follow.
- Quote information was not consistently translated into downstream fulfilment activities.
- Context was lost as work passed between teams, increasing rework and quality risk.
- Exception handling relied heavily on specialist judgement and manual coordination.
- The business needed an evidence-based way to prioritise automation without removing the human judgement required for complex decisions.

The central question was:

> How might we create a shared end-to-end view of the order management journey and identify where automation could reduce effort while preserving transparency, accountability and human oversight?



## My role

As Process Reengineering and Transformation Lead, I led discovery and requirements elicitation. I facilitated workshops, conducted current state analysis, synthesised findings and documented future state workflow opportunities.

I worked with cross functional stakeholders across ordering roles and partnered with the delivery team to translate the workflow direction into an implementation pathway.

My contribution included:

- Facilitating structured discovery with 15+ stakeholders.
- Mapping current state process and task flows across four connected phases.
- Identifying bottlenecks, information gaps, dependencies and manual workarounds.
- Identifying appropriate AI-assisted decision points and human-in-the-loop controls.
- Designing future state workflow patterns for quote authoring, provisioning and exception handling.
- Supporting delivery planning and the business case for staged workflow automation.



## Approach



### 1. Built an end to end view of the work

I brought together stakeholders involved at different points in the order lifecycle to create a structured view across four connected phases:

1. Quote authoring
2. Order capture and translation
3. Order provisioning
4. Exception handling

Rather than treating each step as an isolated task, I used current state process and task flow analysis to examine how work, information and decisions moved between different teams and roles.

This made the difference between the documented process and the work required in practice visible. It also showed stakeholders where upstream choices created downstream effort, delay and uncertainty.

### 2. Identified the systemic friction points

The analysis identified 8+ critical bottlenecks across the end-to-end journey. The following two systemic causes were recurring.

**Manual, email based workflows**  
Teams relied on email to pass information, seek clarification and manage exceptions. This reduced visibility of work in progress, made ownership harder to follow and introduced delays.

**Weak quote to order translation**  
Important context from the quote stage was not consistently carried into provisioning and fulfilment. Downstream teams had to reconstruct information or seek clarification, increasing effort and the potential for error and furthering delays.

The analysis showed that information loss was not simply a documentation issue. It was a workflow design issue: data, responsibility and decision context were fragmented across hand offs.

| Process phase | Friction point | Operational implication | Opportunity area |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quote authoring | Quote information was captured inconsistently and did not always contain the detail needed downstream. | Fulfilment teams had to reconstruct context or request clarification, increasing cycle time and rework risk. | Structure critical quote inputs and carry relevant information forward into the order workflow. |
| Order capture and translation | Information was manually transferred between systems, roles and email threads. | Ownership and work status were difficult to track; details could be missed or duplicated. | Introduce structured hand-offs, workflow visibility and clearer ownership rules. |

| Order provisioning | Teams relied on email to seek clarification, coordinate work and manage missing information. | Progress slowed when individuals were unavailable; dependencies were difficult to see or manage. | Route work to the appropriate queue, surface missing information early and make dependencies visible. |
| Order provisioning | Work was not always prioritised consistently across orders, tasks and exceptions. | Urgent or high-impact work could compete with routine tasks without a transparent prioritisation method. | Introduce workflow rules that support prioritisation, routing and status visibility. |

| End-to-end | Responsibility, decision context and information were fragmented across hand-offs. | Teams lacked a shared view of the overall journey, making it difficult to identify root causes or prioritise improvements. | Establish an end-to-end workflow model with shared data, explicit decision points and measurable hand-offs. |

### 3. Designed connected future state workflows

I mapped future state fulfilment workflows across quote authoring, order provisioning and exception handling.

The design focused on improving information continuity, clarifying decision points and reducing avoidable manual coordination. It considered where structured workflow automation could assist with:

- Capturing and carrying forward relevant quote information.
- Routing work to the appropriate role or queue.
- Identifying incomplete, inconsistent or exceptional orders.
- Supporting decisions with relevant context and recommendations.
- Escalating exceptions that required human review.
![Future-state concept showing structured inputs, routing, decision support, human exception review and feedback loops.](/portfolio/images/work/sales-order-management/future-state-concept.png)

*Future-state concept: automation supports repeatable coordination while complex exceptions retain human review.*


### 4. Defined human in the loop AI controls

The objective was not to automate every decision. Some exceptions required specialist judgement, accountability and contextual understanding.

I identified opportunities for AI-assisted decision-making rather than fully autonomous workflow execution. The future state model retained human review at key checkpoints, particularly when decisions required interpretation, escalation or exception management.

This created a practical programme principle:

> Automate repeatable coordination and information handling; retain human accountability for decisions that require judgement, context or escalation.

![Human-in-the-loop decision model distinguishing work that can be automated, AI-assisted or escalated for human review.](/portfolio/images/work/sales-order-management/human-loop.png)

*Decision model: automate repeatable work, assist with context and recommendations, and escalate high-judgement exceptions.*


### 5. Connected discovery to delivery planning

The future state workflow artefacts gave the delivery team a shared foundation for planning design and build activity. They provided a structured way to decide what to automate first, where dependencies existed and which decisions needed further validation.

I partnered with the delivery team to shape an implementation approach that connected discovery outputs to a staged transformation pathway:

1. Establish workflow foundations.
2. Prioritise automation candidates.
3. Validate and build.
4. Measure, learn and iterate.


## Outcomes

The engagement created a shared, evidence-based view of the SD-WAN sales-order journey and established a future state direction for workflow automation. Outcomes included:

- **15+** cross-functional stakeholders engaged through structured discovery.
- **4** end-to-end process phases analysed.
- **8+** critical bottlenecks identified through process and task-flow analysis.
- A staged workflow-automation pathway defined, including AI-assisted decision points and human exception review.
- A material potential efficiency opportunity identified through the transformation programme, subject to implementation and benefits validation.

The work positioned the business to reduce manual effort, strengthen information continuity and improve operational quality without designing out the human judgement needed for complex exceptions.

## Reflection

Transformation programmes often begin with a visible pain point but require deeper systems thinking to address the underlying causes.

Mapping the end-to-end journey made it possible to distinguish local frustrations from structural issues in information flow, decision-making and ownership. The most valuable outcome was not a single workflow diagram. It was a shared, evidence based view that helped stakeholders and delivery teams move from “the process is broken” to a more useful question:

> Which parts should be simplified, automated or supported—and where must human judgement remain?
