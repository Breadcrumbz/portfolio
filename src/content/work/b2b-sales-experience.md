## Context

I supported the design and delivery of an internal B2B sales application used by teams progressing customer acquisition activities.

The work required translating user needs, business requirements and technical constraints into clear, testable and delivery ready experiences. I partnered with product managers, business analysts, subject matter experts and developers across planning, requirements clarification, prototyping, user testing and build support.

Alongside feature work, I contributed to the evolution of the product’s design system and pattern library to improve consistency as the application developed.

## Challenge

Internal sales applications need to balance multiple needs:

- Users need to complete tasks efficiently and with confidence.

- The business needs workflows that support commercial priorities.

- Delivery teams need feasible, well-defined requirements.

- Stakeholders need to make decisions before development effort is committed.

- Design direction needs to be validated with users and supported through implementation.

The core design question was:

> How might we make a complex B2B sales application easier to understand and use, while giving delivery teams the clarity needed to plan, build and iterate with confidence?

## My role

As Digital UX/UI Designer, I supported planning, design and delivery of the B2B sales application.

I worked across discovery, design, validation and implementation support, collaborating closely with product, business and engineering stakeholders.

My contribution included:

- Partnering with product managers, business analysts and subject-matter experts to clarify requirements and priorities.

- Producing wireframes, high-fidelity designs, clickable prototypes and design specifications.

- Presenting design direction to internal teams, consultants and stakeholders.

- Planning and facilitating user testing sessions.

- Synthesising findings into recommendations for backlog prioritisation and product improvement.

- Working with in house designers to maintain the design system and pattern library.

- Supporting developers through build and test cycles to clarify design intent and resolve delivery issues.

## Approach

### 1. Understood the workflow and clarified requirements

The application brought together user needs, business rules and operational requirements.

I worked with product and business stakeholders to clarify what each feature needed to support, then translated those requirements into tangible design artefacts. This made it possible to turn abstract requirements into workflows, interactions and screens that teams could review, challenge and refine.

*B2B sales workflow: a redacted view of the internal process used to clarify requirements and identify experience opportunities.*

### 2. Used design as a shared delivery tool

Wireframes, high fidelity prototypes and presentations created a common reference point for internal teams, consultants and stakeholders.

They supported:

- Story refinement and prioritisation.

- Development estimation.

- Design and technical clarification.

- Stakeholder review.

- Implementation planning.

The aim was not to create documentation for its own sake. It was to make decisions visible early enough for teams to test assumptions and reduce avoidable revision later in delivery.

![Requirements-to-design chain showing a business need translated into a user requirement, wireframe and high-fidelity prototype.](/portfolio/images/work/b2b-sales-experience/requirements-design.png)
*Requirements-to-design: making business and user needs visible early enough for teams to review, estimate and refine.*

### 3. Designed and validated solutions

I developed low and high fidelity prototypes to explore workflows, interactions and interface patterns.

These artefacts helped stakeholders understand proposed solutions and enabled user feedback before features were built. I also created design specifications to support the transition from design to development, giving developers clearer direction on intended interactions and layout behaviour.

### 4. Used research to inform prioritisation

I planned and facilitated 11 user testing sessions to understand how people responded to proposed designs and where friction remained.

I synthesised findings into recommendations that informed iteration, delivery decisions and backlog prioritisation. This kept user evidence connected to product development rather than treating research as a one off activity.

![Prototype feedback loop showing user testing, insight synthesis, design recommendations and backlog prioritisation.](/portfolio/images/work/b2b-sales-experience/prototyping-backlog.png)

*Feedback loop: connecting user evidence to iteration, delivery decisions and backlog priorities.*

### 5. Supported scalable delivery

As features developed, I worked with in-house designers to manage and evolve the design system and pattern library.

This supported a more consistent product experience, enabled reuse of established patterns and reduced design debt. It also created a shared design language that made collaboration with delivery teams more efficient.

### 6. Collaborated through build and test

I worked with developers throughout build and test cycles to validate that implementation reflected the intended design direction.

This involved responding to emerging questions, clarifying specifications and helping triage issues as they arose. The result was a more connected hand off process, with design actively supporting delivery rather than ending at prototype handover.

## Outcome

The work strengthened the connection between user centred design and enterprise delivery for a B2B sales application.

**Research-informed design**  

Used user testing to identify friction and inform recommendations for iterative product improvement.

**Stronger stakeholder alignment**  

Used presentations and high fidelity prototypes to create a shared view of design direction, supporting clearer decisions and fewer avoidable revision cycles.

**Delivery-ready requirements**  

Translated requirements into wireframes, prototypes and design specifications to support story refinement, estimation and sprint delivery.

**More consistent product experience**  

Contributed to design system and pattern library management, supporting reuse, consistency and reduced design debt.

**Implementation support**  

Partnered with developers through build and test, improving the accuracy of design hand-off and supporting delivery issue resolution.

These outcomes describe my contribution within the wider product and delivery team.

## Reflection

Enterprise UX is as much about enabling good decisions as it is about producing polished screens.

Prototypes, specifications and testing created a practical bridge between user needs, commercial objectives and implementation constraints. The most valuable contribution was maintaining that connection through delivery: using design to make requirements visible, test assumptions, support prioritisation and help developers deliver the intended experience.
