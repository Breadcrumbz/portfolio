## Context

I supported an AI-enablement initiative focused on improving how engineers and product teams found, understood and applied organisational AI guidance.

The initial brief was to refresh a developer intranet. Research revealed a broader issue: guidance, approved tools, policies and support channels existed, but were fragmented across sources and difficult to use in the flow of work.

I conducted qualitative research with engineers, product owners and business leaders, translated the findings into a developer-centred information architecture, and created a prototype that informed a refreshed pilot intranet for 170+ developers and engineers.

## Challenge

The organisation wanted to encourage responsible AI adoption across its engineering and product community. However, people encountered several barriers:

- Information about approved AI tools was dispersed across multiple sources.
- Policies and practical guidance were difficult to find and interpret.
- Developers were unclear about where to seek support or clarification.
- Existing information structures did not reflect how developers looked for answers while delivering work.
- The intranet refresh brief did not yet address the wider organisational opportunity for AI enablement.

The core design question was:

> How might we create an accessible, trusted entry point for AI guidance that helps developers make informed decisions without navigating fragmented tools, documents and support channels?

## My role

As Developer Enablement & AI Strategy lead, I conducted research and synthesis, developed the information architecture and created a prototype for a refreshed pilot intranet.

I worked with engineers, product owners and business leaders to understand practical adoption barriers and turn their needs into a structured, accessible experience.

My contribution included:

- Facilitating qualitative research with 15+ engineers, product owners and business leaders.
- Identifying AI-adoption barriers, usability gaps and workflow friction.
- Synthesising research into prioritised experience and information design recommendations.
- Translating technical and business requirements into a developer-friendly information architecture.
- Building a prototype to inform the refreshed pilot intranet.
- Identifying a research-led opportunity for a broader AI centre of excellence model.

## Approach

### 1. Looked beyond the intranet brief

I treated the intranet refresh as a starting point rather than a predefined solution.

Through research sessions, I explored how people currently found guidance, what information they needed at decision points and where the experience broke down. The aim was to understand not only what content was missing, but why existing resources were not consistently supporting adoption.

### 2. Identified the adoption barriers

The research surfaced five recurring themes.

**Fragmentation**  
Guidance, tools, policies and contacts were distributed across multiple channels.

**Uncertainty**  
Developers needed clearer direction on approved tools and responsible-use expectations.

**Discoverability**  
Existing information was difficult to locate at the moment it was needed.

**Practical relevance**  
Participants needed guidance that connected policy and principles to real delivery workflows.

**Support pathways**  
People were not always sure who to contact when questions or edge cases arose.

These findings shifted the discussion from “refresh an intranet” to “create a clearer front door to AI adoption”.

![Research synthesis board showing themes of fragmented guidance, trusted tools, policy clarity, discoverability and support pathways.](/portfolio/images/work/ai-adoption/research-themes.png)

*Research synthesis: the central issue was not a lack of content, but the difficulty of finding trusted, relevant guidance during delivery work.*

### 3. Structured a trusted guidance hub

I synthesised the findings into a developer-friendly information architecture for the pilot intranet.

The structure brought together the core information people needed to navigate AI adoption:

- AI guidance and practical resources.
- Approved tools and related information.
- Policies and responsible-use expectations.
- Key contacts and support pathways.
- Organisational updates and enablement resources.

The goal was to reduce cognitive load and give users a clear path from a question—such as “What tool can I use?” or “Who can help?”—to a reliable answer.

I informed the structure with relevant organisational playbooks and AI-adoption practices, while grounding decisions in needs expressed by research participants.

![Developer-centred information architecture connecting AI guidance, approved tools, responsible-use policy, key contacts and enablement resources.](/portfolio/images/work/ai-adoption/info-architecture.png)

*Information architecture: one coherent route from a developer question to trusted guidance, tooling information or support.*

### 4. Prototyped the experience

I created a prototype to make the information architecture tangible and support stakeholder discussion.

The prototype gave teams a cohesive direction for the refreshed pilot intranet. It enabled review of navigation, hierarchy, content groupings and key user pathways before implementation.

It was not intended to be a final operating model for AI adoption. It was a practical experience artefact: a shared reference point for evaluating the recommendations and moving toward delivery.

### 5. Framed the broader opportunity

Research showed that developer enablement was only one part of the organisation’s AI-adoption challenge. Other functions were likely to have similar needs for trusted tools, policy interpretation, practical guidance and support.

Based on this evidence, I identified an opportunity to extend the initiative into an AI centre-of-excellence model. This was a proposed future direction, not an implemented outcome, intended to connect fragmented enablement efforts and support broader organisational innovation.

## Outcome

The prototype informed a refreshed pilot intranet serving 170+ developers and engineers. It created a more cohesive destination for AI guidance, approved tooling, policy and key contacts.

- **15+** engineers, product owners and business leaders engaged through qualitative research.
- **170+** developers and engineers served by the refreshed pilot intranet.
- **One** cohesive information architecture connecting guidance, tools, policy and support pathways.
- A research-led opportunity framed to extend developer enablement into a potential organisation-wide AI centre of excellence.

The pilot intranet and associated adoption outcomes were delivered through the wider programme. This case study describes my contribution to research, synthesis, information architecture and prototype delivery.

## Reflection

Adoption challenges are rarely solved by adding more content. The more important question is whether people can find trusted, relevant guidance at the moment they need it.

The research changed the scope of the conversation. Rather than treating an intranet as the goal, it established a clearer enablement problem: people needed one accessible, credible route to the tools, guidance, policy and support required for responsible AI use.
